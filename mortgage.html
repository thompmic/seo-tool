Four decisions. One steadier purchase.

A home purchase has deadlines, documents, and costs that are easy to miss when every decision feels urgent. Use this checklist to prepare the right questions and keep the next step visible.

Get pre-approved

Gather income records, asset statements, and identity and housing-history details. Learn what the lender actually verified, what conditions remain, and when your letter expires.

  • Know what distinguishes a preliminary estimate from a document-reviewed pre-approval.
  • Set a payment limit that leaves room for taxes, insurance, and repairs.

Budget the full cost

Plan for closing charges, mortgage insurance where applicable, property taxes, association dues, and the repairs and setup costs that arrive with ownership.

  • Compare your Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure.
  • Ask what PMI protects, how it is priced, and what cancellation rules apply.

Protect the loan file

Keep your finances steady through underwriting. A new debt, job change, or undocumented deposit can prompt questions about your ability to repay and the source of closing funds.

  • Know what to call your lender about before it happens.
  • Verify cash-to-close and wire instructions with the closing agent.

Walk the home carefully

Check included appliances, plumbing, visible water damage, agreed repairs, alarms, access, and the main water shut-off. Document concerns and raise them before signing.

  • Use the walkthrough to confirm condition against the contract.
  • Know where to report a problem and how to record the agreed remedy.

Estimate principal and interest.

Enter a home price, down payment, rate, and term. This estimate covers principal and interest only; taxes, insurance, mortgage insurance, HOA dues, and utilities are additional.

Use your lender’s Loan Estimate for a quote. Rates and eligibility depend on your loan and circumstances.
